# Particle-based Generalised Stochastic Optimisation 

## Jiechen Jackie Zhang and O. Deniz Akyildiz Department of Mathematics, Imperial College London 

> {jackie.zhang21, deniz.akyildiz}@imperial.ac.uk

## August 5, 2026 

Abstract 

We develop a class of diffusion-based stochastic particle optimisation methods for loss functions with intractable gradients. Specifically, we consider problems in which the loss gradient is an integral with respect to a parameter-dependent distribution, a structure that includes training generative models, fine-tuning, and learning latent-variable models. We introduce mean-field dynamics and its interacting-particle approximations, which contain several existing algorithms as special cases and provides a route to constructing new methods. Under well-posedness and joint contractivity assumptions, we prove exponential convergence and show that the continuous-time particle system admits a non-asymptotic error bound. We illustrate it by developing momentum and higher-order Langevin variants and evaluating them on maximum marginal-likelihood estimation and energy-based-model training. 

# 1 Introduction 

Many problems in statistics and machine learning can be formulated as the optimisation of a loss function ℓ : Θ → R whose gradient takes the form of an expectation of a (stochastic gradient) function 

F (θ, x ) with respect to an intractable parameter-dependent distribution πθ , i.e. ∇θ ℓ(θ) = Eπθ [F (θ, X )] .This structure arises in problems such as maximum-likelihood training of energy-based models ( EBM s) (Hinton, 2002; Nijkamp et al., 2019; Song and Kingma, 2021; Oliva et al., 2025), latent EBM s (Pang et al., 2020; Marks et al., 2026), maximum marginal likelihood estimation ( MMLE ) (Atchadé et al., 2017; De Bortoli et al., 2021; Kuntz et al., 2023; Lim et al., 2024; Akyildiz et al., 2025), and fine tuning (Marion et al., 2025). These problems are usually solved via generic first-order optimisation schemes where each iteration involves running Markov chain Monte Carlo ( MCMC ) to sample from πθ, resulting in a nested optimisation-sampling scheme (Younes, 1999; Andrieu and Vihola, 2014; Atchadé et al., 2017; De Bortoli et al., 2021). However, this is computationally expensive with delicate convergence properties dependent on various factors such as Markov-chain bias, mixing, and step-size selections. A number of algorithms have been developed in recent literature to avoid MCMC sampling via the use of interacting particles, see, e.g. Kuntz et al. (2023); Lim et al. (2024); Akyildiz et al. (2025) for MMLE in latent variable models, and Marion et al. (2025) for fine tuning of generative models. These methods construct joint sampling-optimisation procedures, typically with Langevin processes and gradient descent based optimisers. However, these methods are often developed for particular choices of optimisers and diffusions which are often inferior in performance to more sophisticated methods. In fact, practical applications of such methods often use modern optimisers such as Adam (Kingma and Ba, 2015), see, e.g., Kuntz et al. (2023); Marks et al. (2026) or preconditioned Langevin dynamics, see, e.g., Glyn-Davies et al. (2025); Wang et al. (2026); Marks et al. (2026). This raises the question of how to 1arXiv:2608.02844v1 [stat.ML] 3 Aug 2026 define principled, general purpose stochastic optimisation methods for loss functions with intractable gradients. In this work, we develop general diffusion-based particle optimisation methods based on a continuous mean-field dynamics. Specifically: (C1) We develop a general diffusion-based framework for stochastic optimisation of loss functions, accounting for general optimisation and sampling dynamics. This unifies and generalises several existing methods for general optimisation problems whose gradients involve parameter-dependent expectations and leads to the design of a novel class of algorithms. (C2) Under this general formulation, we establish exponential contraction of the mean-field system under a joint strong-monotonicity condition and finite-N error bounds for its particle approx-imation. Under an additional conservative, jointly strongly convex structure, the stationary parameter is the unique global minimiser of the loss. (C3) Using the general framework, we develop two novel algorithms, (i) a higher-order Langevin dynamics based MMLE algorithm, and (ii) a momentum-based algorithm for training EBM s. We show that these algorithms outperform existing methods in their respective applications. We also discuss how to develop novel algorithms for other applications, such as fine tuning of generative models, by instantiating the general framework with appropriate choices of optimisers and diffusions. The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section 2, we provide background on the optimisation problems we consider, and the sampling dynamics we use, together with a discussion of related work. In Section 3, we introduce our general mean-field system for stochastic optimisation. We then provide theoretical results on the convergence of the mean-field system and finite-particle error bounds in Section 4. Finally, we show the design of novel algorithms in Section 5 accompanied with numerical results and experiments.

## 2.2 Related Work 

We compare related methodologies which also solve the stochastic optimisation problem, with most directly related to objectives of the form in (1). Classical approaches use the stochastic approximation (SA ) framework of Robbins and Monro (1951) with Markovian noise, where for each outer optimisation step on the parameters, an inner loop MCMC sampler is used to approximate samples from πθ and the resulting Monte Carlo ( MC ) average is plugged into a first-order update (Younes, 1999; Andrieu and Vihola, 2014; Atchadé et al., 2017). This includes Stochastic Optimization via Underdamped Langevin ( SOUL ) and its more general kernel counterparts ( SOUK ), where several Langevin or more general Markov-kernel steps are used per parameter update (De Bortoli et al., 2021). In EBM training, Contrastive Divergence ( CD -k) runs k short MCMC transitions, typically initialised from data, to approximate the negative-phase expectation (Hinton, 2002); Persistent Contrastive Divergence ( PCD )instead keeps a persistent particle cloud and usually applies one or a few transitions per parameter step, reducing the nested-loop cost at the price of non-equilibrium bias (Tieleman, 2008; Oliva et al., 2025). Related single-loop schemes make this persistence explicit. Implicit Diffusion ( ImpDiff ) couples the parameter dynamics with one sampler update step per optimisation step, so that particles and parameters evolve jointly (Marion et al., 2025). When the inner approximation is based on Sequential Monte Carlo ( SMC ), Cuin et al. (2026) maintain weighted particles. The problem of MMLE of a latent variable model pθ(x, y ) fits directly in the setting of (1) after applying Fisher’s identity to obtain an expectation under the posterior pθ(· | y). The classical Expectation-Maximisation ( EM ) algorithm alternates an E-step, which forms or approximates the posterior expectation, with an M-step in θ (Dempster et al., 1977). When the E-step is intractable, this leads to Monte Carlo, stochastic approximation, online, variational, and biased-MCMC variants of  

> EM

(Wei and Tanner, 1990; Delyon et al., 1999; Cappé and Moulines, 2009; Fort and Moulines, 2003; Gruffaz et al., 2024; Cuin et al., 2025). An alternate approach introduces a particle approximation to the optimisation dynamics and avoids separately equilibrating an inner Markov chain. For MMLE ,Particle Gradient Descent ( PGD ) performs a coupled continuous-time descent of the EM free energy introduced by Neal and Hinton (1998) over parameters and a variational posterior approximation, using Langevin dynamics on the particles for current posterior approximations (Kuntz et al., 2023). Momentum Particle Gradient Descent ( MPGD ) adds momentum to both the parameter and particle dynamics, yielding an underdamped extension of this coupled system (Lim et al., 2024). The Interacting Particle Langevin Algorithm ( IPLA ) instead injects noise into the parameter dynamics and interprets 3the full parameter-particle system as an interacting Langevin diffusion (Akyildiz et al., 2025), with kinetic variants such as kinetic interacting particle Langevin Monte Carlo ( KIPLMC ) extending this perspective to underdamped dynamics (Oliva and Akyildiz, 2024) and adaptations to latent energy-based models (Marks et al., 2026). Whilst these works are mostly developed for MMLE or closely related latent-variable problems, they illustrate the same continuous-time particle optimisation principle, replacing a costly inner MCMC loop with a persistent interacting particle system. Our contribution abstracts and generalises this perspective. Rather than fixing a particular optimiser, sampler, or problem, we formulate a continous-time mean-field optimisation-sampling system for general objectives with gradients of the form (1). This includes existing particle methods such as  

> PGD

and MPGD as special cases, while also allowing more general optimiser dynamics and sampler diffusions.

## 6.1 Limitations and Future Work 

The theory relies on global contractivity and uniform-moment assumptions, which can be restrictive for the non-convex objectives encountered in large neural models. Moreover, the guarantees concern the continuous-time systems: establishing finite-step error and stability for the proposed splitting schemes requires a separate discretisation analysis. The time-dependent contraction result is conditional on verifying its monotonicity rate for each schedule. Future work should therefore develop local or non-convex guarantees, analyse discretisation and adaptive preconditioning.

## C.1 MMLE with Higher-Order Langevin Dynamics 

Here we discuss the Bayesian image-deblurring experiments introduced in Section 5.1. Given a blurred and noisy observation y = Bx + ε, obtained through an ill-conditioned blurring operator B and

We equip this ill-conditioned inverse problem with a total-variation prior, with strength governed by an unknown scalar parameter θ.The hyper-parameter θ typically requires manual tuning but instead we estimate an optimal value by framing this as a high-dimensional MMLE problem. With the total-variation prior, we have a non-differentiable posterior over the latent image which closely follows the setup in Section 5.3 of Encinar et al. In particular, B is a uniform local averaging operator taking the value of an interior pixel x⋆ij and replacing with the uniform average of a P × P

neighbourhood of pixels, with truncation and renormalisation at the image boundaries. We equip the latent image x⋆ with the anisotropic total-variation log-prior,

The reason we decide the interweaving updates in (24) is one of computational efficiency. The computational bottleneck in these schemes is usually the expensive computations and evaluations of the gradients of the energy and objective functions, with the cost of such gradient evaluations also scaling with the size of the model. We observe in (24) and other second order schemes that gradient evaluations are required twice in one iteration- once before the A step and once after. However this issue may be circumvented by saving the second gradient evaluation at the end of one iteration and re-using it for the first gradient evaluation at the start of the next iteration, essentially only requiring one gradient evaluation per iteration. Note that this is true provided the A1,A2 updates are sequential between the two sets of B1,B2 updates. This would not be possible if we were to naively update particles and parameters in series. We would also like to mention that our scheme admits other formulations such as using the BAOAB scheme with the same benefits as our proposed OBABO-like scheme and may also be written in the same way. Whilst other practical integrators for ULD are known and widely used, such as the (Euler) Exponential Integrator used in the MPGD discretisation (Lim et al., 2024), we found empirically that the OBABO-like splitting scheme to be the most stable and performative whilst admitting larger step-sizes. Even at the level of the full coupled system, the second-order full-system dynamics admits a natural decomposition into interpretable subcomponents that can be updated separately, which motivates the use of a splitting scheme.
