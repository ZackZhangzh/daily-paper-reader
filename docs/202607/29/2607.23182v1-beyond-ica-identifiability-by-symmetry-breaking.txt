# Beyond ICA: Identifiability by Symmetry Breaking

## Abstract 

We prove the identifiability of deep generative models (DGMs) with piecewise-affine (PWA) decoders and Gaussian mixture model (GMM) priors, in a purely unsupervised setting. We introduce three algebraic contrast principles for sym-metry breaking: domain contrast , which trivializes the mixture symmetry group; 

mechanism contrast , which ensures every decoder branch is witnessed by a unique boundary; and interaction contrast , which forbids parameter conspiracies between latent components and decoder branches. Together they exploit the interplay be-tween the discrete combinatorics of the PWA map and the continuous symmetry structure of the latent GMM. Continuity is replaced by algebraic symmetry con-ditions; injectivity is decoupled from structural identification and required only for pointwise inversion. Our results form a hierarchy: from law identifiability 

(LID; latent distribution up to a global affine map) through map identifiability 

(MID; decoder up to the same map) to posterior and pointwise identifiability . The ICA-form ambiguity emerges under conditions on diagonal component covariances. Assumptions are only on the data-generating process, not on learning methods, except for the interaction contrast. To our knowledge this is the first to develop algebraic symmetry-breaking into an identifiability engine for a concrete nonlinear model class, the first to admit discontinuous decoders, and the first to handle fully non-injective decoders, where every observation admits multiple latent codes. 

## 1 Introduction 

Identifiability is the foundation of trustworthy DGM. A deep latent variable model X = f (Z) is 

identifiable if the observable law 1 of X pins down both the generator f and the latent law of Z, up to a well-defined ambiguity class. Without it, two trainings on the same data can converge to entirely different mechanisms, and downstream tasks lose any guarantee of being well-posed. Identifiability is the precondition for robust, interpretable, and disentangled representations [ 26 ], with deep classical roots in finite mixtures [ 2 , 38 , 43 ], tensor decompositions [ 23 ], and linear ICA [ 7 , 8, 35 ]. The nonlinear, high-capacity setting of modern generators makes the ambiguity class far larger than these classical settings could admit [19]. Nonlinear ICA breaks ambiguity by domain contrast—but only under observed domain labels. The dominant remedy treats data as drawn from several domains —variations in the problem setting (gender, temperature, time, location)—each shaping the latent law inside a parametric family, and lets the diversity of domain parameters break the symmetries that otherwise plague the model: in time-contrastive learning (TCL) [ 18 ] domains are time segments; in identifiable variational autoencoder (iVAE) [ 21 ], observed auxiliaries. In both, the domain label is observed. Real data often arrives without labels, so we model the latent law as a GMM—each component being one domain. Going from observed to unobserved domains is qualitatively harder: domain contrast alone no longer pins down spurious solutions.

Mechanism contrast turns branch boundaries into a resource. We add a complementary symmetry-breaking lever from the generator itself. ReLU networks are continuous PWA maps [ 29 ] and achieve almost-optimal convergence rates on discontinuous target functions [ 20 ]. A PWA generator partitions the latent space into branches with distinct affine rules—discrete mechanisms —and each domain is acted on by every mechanism whose branch domain it intersects . The algebraic resource is the branch-boundary structure: when branches do not all share their boundaries, each toggle localises distinct constraints that no smooth global generator could ever produce. Discontinuity of f is fully allowed by our theory but is not what does the work—a continuous PWA already exhibits the boundary structure we exploit. Algebraic symmetry collapse replaces continuity and smoothness. Existing nonlinear-ICA proofs are differential at heart—log-density and Jacobian comparisons [ 13 , 14 , 18 , 21 ]—silent on discontinuous maps and chamber-boundary structure, and structurally requiring smoothness. We replace differential techniques with an algebraic engine: collisions between two candidate models are read as nontrivial elements of an affine symmetry group of the latent law, and assumptions that trivialise this group force the two models to coincide. Three contrast principles—one each for map, law, and their interaction—power the engine (Table 1; formal in §3). The combination yields the headline best of both worlds : the affine link is built from map-side boundary information, so domain labels can remain latent. Group/symmetry arguments have emerged in identifiability since the 1950s [ 17 ]; classical models had small, obvious symmetry groups [ 3], so a general algebraic engine became necessary only with the nonlinear, high-capacity regime. Generative modelling is not signal processing. Classical ICA inherits its goals from signal processing: invert the source pointwise. This requires injectivity, a constraint that is both restrictive theoretically and almost never enforced in mainstream nonlinear-ICA learning procedures [ 13 , 18 , 21 ], even when their theorems demand it. Deep generative modelling cares about a different object: the mechanism 

(the decoder) and the posterior (the representation). Neither needs pointwise inversion. Our hierarchy (law → map → posterior → pointwise; Table 2) delivers the first three without injectivity; pointwise recovery is an optional final upgrade. No form of latent independence is needed for the affine link: Theorems 5 and 8 hold for arbitrary component covariances. Only the ICA-form refinement (Cor. 11) requires diagonal component covariances, i.e., conditional independence of coordinates given the mixture component.

A comprehensive comparison along all assumption axes is in Table 4 (App. C); detailed technical comparisons with iVAE [ 21 ], Kivva et al. [22] , and boundary-richness vs. injectivity appear in Appendix S. Here we highlight the lineages most relevant to our positioning. 

Nonlinear ICA. TCL [ 18 ] and iVAE [ 21 ] identify smooth invertible mixings of independent latents whose marginals vary across observed segments or auxiliaries. In effect, our (MST) distils the algebraic essence of the “diverse domains” variability that powers TCL and iVAE—and here the domains are latent rather than observed labels (Appendix N). Multi-view nonlinear ICA [ 13 ] uses several full mixings of a shared independent latent under a sufficiently-distinct-views condition. Independent mechanism analysis (IMA) [ 14 ] postulates Jacobian-column orthogonality of a smooth invertible mixing and is implemented through normalising flows that are invertible by construction. The mechanism-sparsity line [ 4 , 5 , 24 , 28 , 44 ] places interaction conditions on smooth invertible decoders. All are differential at heart and assume smooth, typically invertible generators. 

Relaxing injectivity and continuity. To our knowledge, only three works in this lineage (partially) relax injectivity. Kivva et al. [22] require injectivity on an f -image region for LID and global injectivity for MID; Matthes et al. [28] focus on local disentanglement under local injectivity; Roeder et al. [31] obtain linear identifiability for discriminative softmax-form models—a different paradigm from generative pushforwards. Continuity of f is dropped only by us; Kivva et al. [22] rely on continuity for density matching to obtain MID, though allow discontinuity for LID. 

Group symmetry in identifiability and disentanglement. Higgins et al. [16] define disentanglement via a given direct-product decomposition of a group on world states; the latent law plays no role and there is no theorem for any concrete model class. Indeterminacy in Generative Models [41 ]taxonomises model indeterminacies as A(F) ∩ A (Pz ); it requires injective generators and resolves multi-domain ambiguity by observed domain labels, and handles the GMM analogue only by collapsing each domain to a single Gaussian (their §4.3, Thm. 4.5)—bypassing rather than trivialising the within-domain symmetry that our (MST) addresses. Ahuja et al. [1] place structure on known 

latent dynamics: shared equivariances of the dynamics define the ambiguity class, with smooth invertible observation map throughout. Group ideas long appear in invariant inference [10 , 12 ], starting from a prescribed group action. 

PWA + GMM, and multi-mechanism modelling. Kivva et al. [22] opened the unsupervised PWA+GMM regime; Xu et al. [42] extend to potentially-degenerate GMMs via encoder sparsity (still injective continuous PWA); Lopez et al. [27] specialise Kivva to comparative DGMs with an observed 

contrastive split. Generalised PCA [ 39 ] and clusterwise linear regression [ 15 ] are remote relatives— single-condition projective settings without unobserved domain labels or branch analysis. Modelling generating processes via multiple temporal or spatial mechanisms is classically well-motivated: temporal exemplars include switching/hybrid systems [ 30 ], recurrent switching linear dynamics [ 25 ], and PWA neuroscience dynamics [ 9]; spatial exemplars include free-form-deformation and PWA image registration [ 11 , 32 , 36 , 37 ]. None addresses identifiability; we bring algebraic identifiability to this PWA modelling tradition. 

## 3 Setup and preparations 

A latent random variable Z follows a GMM and is observed through an unknown PWA map f ; that is, X = f (Z).2 Both the map and the latent law are hidden: only the data of X is available. We ask whether Z and f can be (partially) recovered from this data.

To analyze (1) , we decompose the observation space into regions where each map acts with constant branch structure. This converts the global PWA pushforward equality into a local matching of finite Gaussian mixtures.

## 4 Theoretical results 

Full proofs of all results appear in Appendix A; proof sketches for the key results are given below. Illustrated examples are given in Appendix K and L. 

Proof strategy. Our whole proof strategy has an algebraic flavour and is built around bijections 

between the truth and estimator, at progressively higher levels. (PI) gives a branch-component-pair bijection Ψ. (SB) restricts Ψ to a single boundary and reads off a component-level affine link α: it “matches”–via the induced component bijection –every estimator component to a truth component 

and “stitches” the latent components together by this same α, yielding LID. (USB) gives a branch bijection : it “matches” every estimator branch to a truth branch via its own simple boundary. (MST) upgrades the branch bijection to a global branch-level affine α: it “stitches” across all branches by trivialising the mixture symmetry group, completing MID.

4.2 Law identifiability 

We now tackle the first nontrivial step in the hierarchy: law identifiability (LID) , where we show the two latent GMMs are related by a global affine map. This requires a boundary-genericity condition—the mechanism contrast of Table 1. 

Condition (SB) (Simple boundary). A PWA map h satisfies (SB) if either (a) h has a single chamber (i.e., h is globally affine; Lemma 12), or (b) there exist adjacent chambers (C, C ′) of h and a branch i⋆

that is the unique branch leaving or entering ( Ih(C) \ I h(C′) = {i⋆} or Ih(C′) \ I h(C) = {i⋆}). (SB) constrains only one direction of the boundary crossing (leaving or entering); the opposite direction is unrestricted. Thus a boundary with one branch leaving and three branches entering qualifies as simple. Note: (SB) is satisfied even if multiple branches overlap across many boundaries, provided there exists at least one directional boundary transition where the active branch set changes by exactly one element. (SB) is necessary : Appendix K.1 gives a case where (PI) holds yet (SB) fails and LID breaks. 

Remark 4.4 (Genericity of (SB)) . The violation of (SB) for a strict PWA map is a rigid condition: 

every boundary must have at least two branches leaving together and at least two branches entering together. For generic PWA maps under unconstrained parametrizations, each image-space facet is typically traversed by a single branch, making (SB) a mild genericity requirement. 4.3 Map identifiability 

We now address the stronger question: must the maps f and g themselves coincide (up to the latent reparametrization α)? The key challenge is that LID establishes the existence of α via a single 

toggling boundary, but says nothing about other branches. For MID, we must ensure that every branch of g is related to the corresponding branch of f by the same affine link. This requires two additional ingredients: boundary richness (every branch is witnessed at some simple boundary) and symmetry triviality—the domain contrast of Table 1. 

Condition (USB) (Universal simple boundary). A PWA map h satisfies (USB) if either (a) h has a single chamber, or (b) for every branch i ∈ I, there exist adjacent chambers (C, C ′) of h such that i

is the unique leaving branch or the unique entering branch. (USB) is universal for branches : every branch is witnessed at some simple boundary. It does not require every boundary to be simple. (USB) trivially implies (SB). Similar to (SB), (USB) is satisfied even if multiple branches overlap across many boundaries. (USB) is strictly weaker than global injectivity ; instead of weak injectivity [ 22 ], which demands a fragile, globally unopposed 1-active chamber, (USB) relies only on a robust, local combinatorial asymmetry (Appendix L.4&L.7). 

Remark 4.5 (Genericity of (USB)) . Similar to (SB), (USB) is expected to hold generically: its violation requires that for some branch i, every image-space facet of i is simultaneously shared by at 7least one other branch on both the leaving and entering sides—a measure-zero algebraic constraint intertwined with geometric and combinatorial rigidity. We next introduce the symmetry condition that “locks” the identification. Given a GMM with density p, its mixture symmetry group is Gmix (p) := T ∈ Aff( Rd) : T♯ p = p . This group captures all affine bijections that preserve the mixture law as a whole. It can include maps that 

permute components of equal weight (not just maps that fix each component individually; the distinction is important and discussed in Appendix G.3). 

Condition (MST) (Mixture symmetry triviality). A GMM p satisfies (MST) if Gmix (p) = {Id }.From the symmetry standpoint, (MST) is stronger than (PI): (PI) rules out strict-PWA self-maps of the mixture (Remark 4.2), while (MST) further rules out affine self-maps beyond the identity. (MST) is required only for MID and above; likewise K > 1 is needed only there. Indeed, for K = 1 (a single Gaussian), LID is trivial: any two non-degenerate Gaussians on Rd differ by an affine bijection. (MST) is also necessary for MID in a structural sense; see Example 3 for a prototype (“101”) construction. And this mechanism can be extended to any GMM and beyond (Appendix O). With ob-served domain labels, iVAE’s sufficient-variability assumption (iv) implies our (MST) (Appendix N); indeed (MST) is strictly weaker , holding with as few as d + 1 components, where (iv)—which needs 

K > 2d—does not even apply. 

Remark 4.6 (Genericity of (MST)) . For K ≥ 2 components with generically chosen means, co-variances, and weights, (MST) holds. A violation requires either (a) a nontrivial affine map fixing every component individually, which is ruled out by, e.g., affinely independent means (Lemma 22 in Appendix), or (b) a nontrivial affine map permuting two or more components—i.e., the components are partitioned into cycles and the map acts one-to-one within each cycle—which is ruled out by, e.g., distinct weights or distinct covariance determinants (Lemma 21 in Appendix). Other generic and sufficient conditions for (MST) exist; see Appendix I.3 for a summary. All the conditions are algebraically rigid and occur on a measure-zero set. See Figure 5 in Appendix K for an illustration of why case (b) matters.

## 5 Conclusion and prospects 

We have established an identifiability hierarchy for DGMs with PWA decoders and GMM priors, proceeding from LID through MID to posterior and pointwise identification, and finally to ICA-form recovery. Three algebraic contrast principles—domain ((MST)), mechanism ((USB)), and interaction ((PI))—power an engine that replaces both the topological continuity glue of prior work and the auxiliary side information of the iVAE line. Structural identifiability (recovering the decoder and the posterior) is separated from pointwise inversion, which requires injectivity. 

Genericity. All conditions are generic: (PI) and (MST) are violated only on a measure-zero algebraic subset of the relevant parameter space (Appendix F, I; Remark 4.6); same for (SB)/(USB) under unconstrained PWA parametrizations (Remarks 4.4, 4.5). Concretely: if the branch matrices and biases of PWA map f and parameters of GMM pZ are drawn randomly and independently, identifiability holds almost surely. To our knowledge, this is the first setting in which all identifiability assumptions can simultaneously be argued to be generic in the parameter space of the map and/or the latent law, without requiring observed domain labels, injectivity, or continuity. 

Necessity and prospects. All three conditions should be seen as structurally necessary, not merely sufficient for the present proofs. (SB) is necessary: Appendix K.1 gives a fold example, which can be generalized to a family of examples on Rd, where (PI) and (MST) hold yet LID breaks. (MST) is necessary: Appendix O proves that if a GMM admits any nontrivial affine self-symmetry, it admits a finite-order one; such torsion symmetries can be localised into strict PWA self-maps breaking identifiability. Analogous torsion-based necessity phenomena hold for broad exponential-family and location-scale mixtures (Table 5). We conjecture that the gap between chamber-wise and global (PI) is structurally necessary in the non-injective setting, and that investigating it leads toward a full necessity result for (PI). More broadly, Gaussian mixtures and PWA maps are the first testbed for a 

general algebraic identifiability theory : one can replace the affine group with the natural symmetry group of a general component family F (e.g., any exponential family), study mixtures from F and decoder branches from that F -symmetry group, and ask whether analogues of (PI), (MST), and (USB) again mark the identifiability boundary. 

Causality prospects. For causal discovery, just as identifiability of linear ICA enables the Linear Non-Gaussian Acyclic Model (LiNGAM) [ 34 ], we believe the current identifiability results could enable multi-mechanism-domain causal discovery. For causal inference, in Intact-VAE [ 40 ], which is 9based on domain contrast, a central issue is to ensure that the mechanisms of two treatment arms are identified up to the same affine ambiguity—precisely the problem solved by our MID (Theorem 8).

## L Simple/universal boundaries vs. weak injectivity 

This appendix clarifies how our boundary conditions (SB)/(USB) relate to global injectivity and to Kivva’s weak injectivity [22]. All notation follows the main text. 

L.1 Weak injectivity and 1-active chambers Definition L.1 (Weak injectivity) . A PWA map h : Rd → Rd is weakly injective if there exists a nonempty open set U ⊂ Rd on which every y ∈ U has a unique preimage. 

Proposition 26. Let h be a reduced PWA map with invertible branch rules. Then h is weakly injective if and only if some chamber C satisfies |I h(C)| = 1 .Proof. (⇒) Let U be the set witnessing weak injectivity. For y ∈ U , unique preimage forces

gives a unique preimage on U := C.

L.2 Global injectivity implies both USB and weak injectivity

L.7 Heuristic: (SB)/(USB) is milder than weak injectivity 

The examples above prove logical independence between (SB)/(USB) and weak injectivity. We now argue heuristically that (SB)/(USB) is the milder requirement: it fails only on a higher-codimension set than weak injectivity, is generic once discontinuities are allowed, and is plausibly generic even when the decoder is constrained to be continuous. Throughout, “non-generic” here means failing on a set that—while sometimes of positive measure—shrinks as the branch count grows , not the measure-zero notion used for (PI)/(MST) in the main text. 

Discontinuous decoders. When discontinuities are allowed, (SB) is generic: a single non-co-toggling boundary suffices, and a generic PWA map has one. Its failure set (every boundary co-toggling) is of higher codimension than that of weak injectivity, so (SB) is strictly harder to violate. Nothing beyond the examples above is needed here. 

Continuous decoders (open). When the decoder is constrained to be continuous we have no gener-icity proof for (SB). In one dimension (SB) can fail through an every-boundary folding construction; as this is a condition on slope signs it carves out a set of positive measure, so (SB) is not generic in the strict sense. But the construction needs at least two folds, so (SB) holds with high probability and its failure probability decays exponentially in the branch count M . The construction is one-dimensional; whether it extends to higher dimensions is open—co-toggling has more directions to exploit there—so we expect, but do not show, that a comparable bound survives. 37 Weak injectivity is non-generic. Weak injectivity fails in both regimes, but the strength of the failure depends on the chamber type. 

Bounded chambers (exponentially unlikely). Fix branch count M and pretend that on each chamber C

the event “branch i is active” occurs independently with probability p ∈ (0 , 1) . Then 

Pr( |I f (C)| = 1) = M p (1 − p)M −1 M →∞

provided p stays bounded away from 0—equivalently, the average branch coverage grows with M , as when bounded branch images tile a fixed region ever more densely (a covering/packing argument). Under that condition 1-active chambers, hence weak injectivity, become exponentially rare. 

Unbounded chambers (non-generic, but not exponentially rare). On unbounded tails the coverage need not grow, and the failure is milder. In a 1-D continuous PWA map the two unbounded branches have slopes aL, a R: if sign( aL) = sign( aR) their images run to opposite infinities (1-active tails), otherwise to the same infinity (2-active tails). Under a symmetric random-sign model, tail weak injectivity already fails with probability ≈ 12 —a positive-measure event, not a measure-zero coincidence. This unbounded case is the most favourable one for weak injectivity, and even here it is non-generic. The upshot: weak injectivity demands a global image-exclusivity event—one branch dominating an open region unopposed—which is fragile, and exponentially so on bounded chambers. (SB)/(USB) demand only a local combinatorial asymmetry at a single boundary, which is robust and, once discontinuities are allowed, generic.

The three routes reflect different trade-offs: • USB + MST (Lemma 7) uses geometric boundary structure to isolate each branch, and a single symmetry condition to kill the ambiguity. ## R Additional Discussion 

Boundary richness vs. algebraic conditions. The main-text MID results use (USB) (every branch has an exposed boundary) to establish the branch bijection, illustrating how geometric structure can substitute for algebraic conditions. This is not the only route: Appendix Q presents two alternative theorems that replace (USB) with purely algebraic conditions on the map–latent pair— orbit disjointness ((OD); Theorem 31) and transporter-orbit disjointness ((TOD); Theorem 32). These approaches require weight conditions or stronger orbit conditions instead of boundary richness, illustrating the modularity of the framework. 42 Globalness, not canonicity, of α. Necessity of assumptions. All three conditions are structurally necessary, not merely sufficient for the present proofs. For Gaussian mixtures, Appendix O proves that if a GMM admits any nontrivial affine self-symmetry, then it admits a finite-order one (i.e., torsion ). In globally injective piecewise decoder classes that are closed under precompositions, such torsion symmetries can be localised into law-preserving discontinuous precompositions, producing observationally identical yet mechanism-distinct models. Concretely, a torsion T can give a two-piece construction S = {T, Id }

(details omitted here for scope) that yields a non-(PI) strict PWA self-map S of the kind discussed in Remark 4.2, which explains why such S breaks identifiability. This is the mechanism behind the necessity of (MST). We also have complementary results (see Table 5), showing analogous torsion-based necessity phenomena for broad classes of exponential-family and location-scale mixtures, and further examples (omitted here) showing that the same obstruction can persist even in continuous piecewise classes. Boundary richness is likewise necessary: Appendix K.1 gives a simple fold example in which latent (MST) and (PI) both hold, yet (SB) fails and law identifiability breaks. 

Towards a broader algebraic identifiability theory. Gaussian mixtures and piecewise-affine maps are the first concrete testbed for a wider program. In the present paper, the relevant ambiguity group is the affine group Aff( Rd), which is exactly the natural symmetry group of the Gaussian family. More generally, one can replace affine maps by the natural invertible transformations that preserve a chosen component family F , and then study mixtures from F together with piecewise maps whose branch rules come from that family symmetry group. We also have a group-theoretic proof of (MST) genericity (omitted for scope) that uses weaker conditions than any sufficient conditions shown in Appendix I and applies to many exponential-family mixtures. A key next step is to formulate family-level analogues of parameter injectivity and to understand how far the branch class can be enlarged before hidden symmetries reappear. Our expectation is that analogues of (PI), (MST), and (USB) will again mark the boundary between identifiability and non-identifiability for a much wider class of piecewise-parametric latent variable models. 

## S Technical comparisons with related work 

This section gathers technical comparisons that are too detailed for the main text. 

Two roles of (MST): comparison with prior work. The condition (MST) plays a role analogous to two distinct mechanisms in prior identifiability frameworks: In iVAE [ 21 ], auxiliary labels (observed class indices) serve as tags that allow local identifiability to extend across the latent space. In our setting, (MST) achieves a similar effect: the mixture components serve as internal tags, and (MST) ensures their geometry is rich enough to pin down all branch-wise ambiguities. In Kivva et al. [22 ], MID is obtained by a different mechanism: continuity forces adjacent affine pieces to agree on their common facet, so their relative map fixes a hyperplane. The remaining local alternatives are shear-type or reflection-type. Global density equality, analytic continuation, and GMM identifiability rule out the shear case, since a nontrivial shear cannot preserve a nondegenerate Gaussian mixture. Injectivity then rules out the reflection/fold case. By contrast, our (MST) assumption bypasses this continuity-plus-injectivity argument by directly trivializing the affine self-symmetry group of the latent mixture. Example 2 (Appendix K), adapted from Kivva et al. [22 , Example D.2 ], concretely illustrates how both (PI) and (MST) fail simultaneously, yielding a strict-PWA self-map. 

Comparison with iVAE. Under the PWA+GMM model class, the assumptions of iVAE [ 21 ] are strictly stronger than our (MST), (USB), and chamber-wise (PI): injectivity of the map implies (USB) and chamber-wise (PI) (Appendix M), while observed domain labels together with iVAE’s sufficient-43 variability condition (assumption (iv)) imply (MST) (Appendix N). Concretely: observed labels fix component identities so any admissible symmetry must be component-preserving, reducing (MST) to (JST); generic mean/variance variation then trivialises the remaining within-component stabiliser, giving (JST) and hence (MST). Since our main results use global (PI), which is strictly stronger than the chamber-wise version (Appendix E), we cannot say that our full assumption set is strictly weaker than iVAE’s. However, our framework strictly weakens the domain-contrast assumption : (MST) replaces iVAE’s requirement of observed domain labels plus sufficient variability.
